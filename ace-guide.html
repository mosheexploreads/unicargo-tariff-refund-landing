ACE Portal Registration Guide

Step-by-step instructions to register for the US Customs ACE Portal and receive your IEEPA tariff refund via ACH

Updated May 2026
Why ACE registration is required for your refund On February 20, 2026, the US Supreme Court issued an emergency stay in Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump, halting collection of IEEPA tariffs. Importers who paid IEEPA tariffs on entries made between February 4 and February 24, 2026 are eligible for refunds through CBP's CAPE (Claim for Already-Paid Entries) program. CBP processes all CAPE refunds exclusively via ACH transfer — requiring you to have a registered ACE account with an enrolled US bank account.
Phase 1

Prerequisites — Before You Register

Gather these three things before starting your ACE application. Missing any of them will stall the process.

📋

A — CBP Form 5106

Importer ID / Drawback Claimant ID. Must show a current point-of-contact (POC) email — CBP sends your ACE verification code to this address. If your Form 5106 is out of date, file an updated one first.

🏢

B — IOR Number

Your Importer of Record number — either your EIN (Employer Identification Number) or a CBP-assigned IOR number. This must match the IOR on the entries you are claiming a refund for.

🏦

C — US Bank Account

A US bank account capable of receiving NACHA-compliant ACH transfers. CBP does not issue paper checks for CAPE refunds. Non-resident importers must use a US correspondent banking arrangement.

POC Email is critical: CBP will send a verification code to the email on file with your CBP Form 5106. If that email is no longer monitored or is incorrect, you will not receive the code and cannot complete registration. Update Form 5106 at your local CBP port of entry before starting.
Phase 2

Registering for ACE — Step by Step

Step 1 — Check if you already have an ACE account

Many importers have an ACE account from prior filings. Before registering a new one, check with your customs broker or freight forwarder. You can also search by IOR number at the CBP portal.

If you already have an ACE account, skip to Step 5 — Enroll Your Bank Account. You do not need to re-register.

Step 2 — Go to the ACE Importer Portal Application

Navigate to the official CBP application page:

https://www.cbp.gov/trade/automated/how-to-use-ace/portal-applying

From there, go to the importer registration form:

https://ace-accounts.cbp.gov/s/importer-form

Have your IOR number and business details ready before you begin.

Step 3 — Receive and enter your verification code

After submitting the form, CBP will send a verification code to the email address on your CBP Form 5106. Check that inbox (including spam/junk). Enter the code to verify your account.

Code not received? Your Form 5106 may have an outdated email address. Contact ace.applications@cbp.dhs.gov for assistance.

Step 4 — Complete the ACE application

Fill in your company details, address, IOR number, and contact information. CBP reviews applications typically within 3–5 business days. You will receive an email confirmation when your account is active.

Step 5 — Enroll your US bank account for ACH refunds

Once your ACE account is active, log in and navigate to the ACH Refund section to enroll your US bank account. You will need:

  • Bank name and address
  • ABA routing number (9 digits)
  • Account number
  • Account type (checking or savings)

CBP's ACH Refund enrollment page:

https://www.cbp.gov/trade/automated/how-to-use-ace/ACH-refunds

ACH payments are live: CBP began processing CAPE refunds via ACH on May 12, 2026. Importers with enrolled accounts are receiving funds directly. If your refund is rejected due to banking issues, it will appear in the REV-613 report in your ACE account.

Step 6 — Export your ACE reports for TariffBack

Once your ACE account is active, export the following two reports and send them to TariffBack to begin your refund analysis:

  • ES-001 — Entry Summary report (covers the Feb 4–Feb 24, 2026 date range)
  • ES-003 — Entry Summary Line Item report (same date range)

TariffBack's ACE data export guide walks you through generating these reports — it is included in the email you receive after submitting your audit request.

Multiple IOR Numbers / Suffixes

If your business imported under multiple IOR numbers (e.g., different suffixes for different ports or business units), each IOR may require a separate ACE account enrollment. Common situations:

  • Parent and subsidiary companies with separate EINs
  • Importers who used both their EIN and a CBP-assigned IOR number
  • Businesses that re-organized or changed legal entities during the eligible period

If you are unsure which IOR numbers your entries were filed under, check with your customs broker or review your ES-001 report. TariffBack can help identify the correct IOR configuration during the analysis phase.

Critical Information — May 2026

Only IEEPA tariffs are refundable

Section 301 (China trade war) and Section 232 (steel/aluminum) tariffs are NOT eligible for refund under the CAPE program. Only IEEPA tariffs paid Feb 4–Feb 24, 2026 qualify.

15% replacement tariff is in effect

A new 15% IEEPA tariff took effect after the Supreme Court stay. This is a separate, ongoing charge — not eligible for refund. Your refund is for the prior higher rate paid during the eligible period only.

ACH is the only payment method

CBP does not issue paper checks for CAPE refunds. You must have a US bank account enrolled in ACE via the ACH Refund program to receive your refund.

CAPE filing deadlines apply

CBP may impose a deadline for CAPE Declaration submissions. File as early as possible to ensure your claim is processed. TariffBack recommends completing ACE registration and data export within 30 days.
